How it works

Settled before anyone rings the bell.

I.

You invite

Create your event and choose guests from your contacts. Each receives a personal text from you with their invitation. The Gentry takes care of the rest.

II.

They sign

Each guest reads and signs your waiver on their own phone, at their leisure, days before the evening. Nobody signs anything on a clipboard in the foyer.

III.

The door knows

On arrival, guests scan your Gentry code. Their screen turns green if they're expected and signed, or red if something is missing, bright enough to read from across the room.

A good host never has to ask. With The Gentry, you'll never need to.

Kept between host and guest.

Your guest list belongs to you. Guests see only their own invitations. Hosts see only their own events. Signed waivers are stored securely with the exact text that was signed and when, and they cannot be altered afterward. We never sell your information.

Questions

Frequently asked

Can't a guest just show a screenshot of a green screen?

Not convincingly. The welcome screen shows the guest's name, your event, and a live clock counting the seconds, and the host's own app records every arrival as it happens. A screenshot gives itself away.

Do my guests need the app?

Yes. Guests download The Gentry, sign in with the email you invited, and sign your waiver in a minute or two. They only need to do it once per event.

What does the waiver say?

The Gentry includes a standard release for private social gatherings. In it, guests acknowledge the ordinary risks of a party at a private home, take responsibility for their own consumption, commit to a safe ride home, and release the host from claims arising from ordinary negligence. You can read it in full in the app before you invite anyone.

Does a signed waiver mean I can't be held responsible?

No document can promise that. A signed waiver is a meaningful record that each guest understood and accepted the risks, but the law varies by state and doesn't let anyone waive certain kinds of responsibility, such as serving minors or gross negligence. The Gentry is a courtesy and a record, not legal advice or insurance. Speak with an attorney and your insurance agent about your own situation.

What happens if someone isn't on the list?

Their screen turns red with the reason, such as "Not on the guest list" or "Waiver not signed", so you or your staff can handle it gracefully.

What does it cost?
